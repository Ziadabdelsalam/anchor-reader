# The research behind Anchor

What the evidence says about staying focused on long documents, and what Anchor does about it. The same material is in the app under **Help → Why These Features Work**.

## What works

**1. Break the page into small bites and fade out the rest.**
When text "blurs together", part of the cause is *crowding*: neighbouring letters and lines interfere with the one you're looking at. Extra letter spacing roughly doubled reading accuracy for children with dyslexia and sped up reading by over 20%, and the readers who struggled most gained most.
→ [Zorzi et al. 2012, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3396504)

**2. Use big text and extra letter spacing.**
In an eye-tracking study of 92 people, larger text and wider character spacing made people read significantly faster, with or without dyslexia. The authors recommend at least 18 pt; in a related study most participants preferred the largest size tested (26 pt).
→ [Rello & Baeza-Yates, Universitat Pompeu Fabra](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/e0ca6f40-a0fa-490f-9d03-53d043270deb)

**3. Find *your* font. There is no universal best one.**
Across 352 readers, the gap between a person's fastest and slowest font was 35%, with no loss of comprehension, and different people were fastest in different fonts.
→ [Wallace et al. 2022, ACM TOCHI](https://jeffhuang.com/papers/Readability_TOCHI22.pdf) (UCF, Adobe, Google, Brown)

**4. Work in short sprints with scheduled breaks.**
Students who took breaks whenever they felt like it studied in longer stretches and ended up more tired and less concentrated than students on fixed Pomodoro-style breaks. Micro-breaks reliably raise energy and reduce fatigue.
→ [Biwer et al. 2023, British Journal of Educational Psychology](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/understanding-effort-regulation-comparing-pomodoro-breaks-and-sel) · [Albulescu et al. 2022, PLOS ONE](https://doaj.org/article/c6f37b6ba159469296b2cb968b65505f) · [2025 comparison of break methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/)

**5. Quiz yourself between sections. This is the big one.**
Short recall tests between parts of an online lecture halved mind-wandering (from about 40% of the time), tripled note-taking and improved retention. A major review rated self-testing among the two most effective study techniques, and re-reading and highlighting among the least. In adults with ADHD, prompts to check their own understanding while reading closed the comprehension gap with non-ADHD readers.
→ [Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013, PNAS (Harvard)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/) · [Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/01/130110111734.htm) · [Metacognitive scaffolding & ADHD](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/metacognitive-scaffolding-for-digital-reading-and-mind-wandering-/) · [Eye-Mind Reader](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/eye-mind-reader-an-intelligent-reading-interface-that-promotes-lo/)

**6. Practise noticing when your mind wanders.**
People with more ADHD symptoms mind-wander more, especially on unengaging text, and are less likely to notice when it happens.
→ [University of Waterloo](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/b6000f50-3ae8-4613-a6eb-19002513f983) · [ADD.org summary](https://add.org/coaches-corner-adhd-mind-wandering-and-meta-awareness-what-the-research-says/)

**7. Listen while you read.**
Text-to-speech significantly improved comprehension for students with ADHD-related reading difficulties, and cut reading effort for students with dyslexia.
→ [National Taiwan Normal University](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/the-effects-of-text-to-speech-on-reading-comprehension-in-junior-/)

**8. Put your phone in another room.**
Having your own phone on the desk, even face-down and silent, reduced available working memory in experiments with about 800 people.
→ [Ward et al. 2017, UT Austin](https://news.utexas.edu/2017/06/26/the-mere-presence-of-your-smartphone-reduces-brain-power)

**9. Protect yourself from interruptions.**
Attention on any one screen now lasts about 47 seconds on average, and getting back to a task after an interruption can take close to half an hour.
→ [Gloria Mark, UC Irvine](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/how-sharpen-your-attention-and-meet-your-goals-2024)

**10. Read at your own pace.**
Speed-reading apps that flash words remove the small look-backs that support comprehension; understanding drops as speed rises.
→ [Rayner et al. 2016, Psychological Science in the Public Interest](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/so-much-to-read-so-little-time-how-do-we-read-and-can-speed-readi/)

**11. Prefer dark text on a light background, and moderate line lengths.**
Proofreading is more accurate with dark-on-light text. People prefer about 45–72 characters per line.
→ [Piepenbrock et al., summarized](https://cogsci.nl/blog/is-bright-text-on-a-dark-background-a-good-idea) · [Dyson on line length](https://humanfactors.com/newsletters/optimal_line_length.html)

**12. For documents that really matter, consider paper.**
A meta-analysis of 54 studies found comprehension of informational text is a little better on paper than on screens, especially under time pressure.
→ [Delgado et al. 2018](https://www.uv.es/lasalgon/papers/Delgado%202018%20dont%20throw%20away%20your%20printed%20books.pdf)

## What doesn't hold up (left out on purpose)

- **Bionic Reading**: no speed benefit across 2,074 readers, and none in a 2024 peer-reviewed study. [Readwise](https://blog.readwise.io/bionic-reading-results/) · [VU Amsterdam](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/no-bionic-reading-does-not-work/)
- **Coloured overlays as a treatment**: a systematic review found they can't be endorsed. Anchor offers tints only as a comfort preference. [Griffiths et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/104441/)
- **Speed reading / RSVP**: see point 10.

---

Anchor is a reading aid, not a medical treatment. If reading has become much harder than it used to be, it's worth talking to a doctor; attention, sleep, vision and stress can all play a part.
